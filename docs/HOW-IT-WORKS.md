# How it works

This page describes the whole loop, from an app opening to the response, in plain words.

## The pieces

- **One shortcut**, called PROSOCHĒ.
- **Two Personal Automations** that you make by hand. One runs when one of your chosen apps is opened and hands the shortcut the text `Open`. The other runs when one of those apps is closed and hands it the text `Close`. A shortcut cannot install these for you.
- **One state file**, `PROSOCHE/state.json`, in the Shortcuts folder of your iCloud Drive. This is PROSOCHĒ's memory.
- **One Note**, named PROSOCHĒ, which holds the guide. A second Note, named Capture, exists only if you choose to jot something down as a way out.
- **A manual menu** with four items. Open PROSOCHĒ in the Shortcuts app to see it:
  - Read Me opens the Note.
  - Setup Check runs the first-run questions and prompts again.
  - Toggle Voice turns speaking on or off.
  - Emergency Restore puts the screen, sound and colour back.

## What happens when you open an app

PROSOCHĒ keeps two measures of your recent behaviour.

- The first is short-term. It rises with each open, and rises more when you reopen an app quickly or stay past a time you said. It falls when you keep to a time you said, and it cools as you spend time away.
- The second is the day's volume of opens. The day starts at 4 a.m., not at midnight.

Together, the two measures pick one of nine steps. The nine come in four groups, described here by what you see:

1. **Nothing at all.** This is most opens.
2. **A light touch, with no questions.** A one-tap pause where you choose Stay or Exit. The screen turns grey for a while. The sound goes quiet for a while.
3. **A question.** It asks how long you mean to stay. Or it reflects back what you said against what happened, and you choose stay, leave, or say how long. Or it takes you straight to somewhere better.
4. **Stepping in.** It sends you back to the Home Screen. Or it speaks the reflection aloud once, only if you allowed speaking when you installed it. Or it starts a cooldown.

Only one response happens per open.

One of the two questions at install sets how quickly PROSOCHĒ steps up from one group to the next. You choose how gentle or strict that is, and the suggested answer is fine to start with.

## Saying how long you'll stay

When PROSOCHĒ asks, you can say how long you mean to stay, in a few words and a time. While you are inside that time, further opens of that kind stay silent. Silence is what you get for saying what you mean to do and keeping to it.

Going over the time, or behaviour that breaks what you said, is noticed at your next open. It is never noticed in the middle of a session, because nothing runs then.

## Ways out

When PROSOCHĒ offers somewhere better to go, the options are another app, a web page, a web search, a Maps search, or a quick note named Capture. The choice rotates. PROSOCHĒ remembers on your phone which ways out you take, and that shapes what it offers next. That is the mechanism, and it is all this page claims about it.

## No dice

The variety in PROSOCHĒ is counter-based, so the same history gives the same response. The shortcut contains exactly one random number, and it only labels a session so that two sessions never share a name. You can count it yourself: [the check](VERIFY.md).

## Screen, sound and colour

PROSOCHĒ can change your screen brightness, your sound volume and a display colour setting.

- Brightness and volume are read and saved before any change, and put back afterwards.
- If a setting's original value cannot be read, that setting is left alone.
- Colour Filters cannot be read back, so every recovery path switches them off.
- Emergency Restore, in the manual menu, is always available and puts all three back.
- These put-back behaviours are still being checked on phones.

## What it keeps

One file and one Note, in your own iCloud, and nothing sent anywhere by PROSOCHĒ. See [Privacy](PRIVACY.md).

## Reading the source

`source/PROSOCHE.xml` is exactly what was signed. You can check that for yourself: [the check](VERIFY.md). The shortcut is generated by a private tool, so the file is a readable record and not something to edit. The variable names inside it are working names, and they differ from the words you see on screen.

It has been tried on iOS 26 only and has not been walked on iOS 27.
