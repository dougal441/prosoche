<objective>
The Aware fork exists for one reason, and an unrelated setting currently deletes it.

`build_sentient.py` inserts the Use Model contract audit at the **first** contract marker in
document order and then breaks. Before Build Addendum 01 (Phase 11 plans 01-06), "first marker"
and "the OPEN-arm marker" were the same action. Plan 11-05 added a second OPEN-arm dispatch
rendering — the arm taken when a user has removed the Panic Escape bypass — and the audit was
not extended to it. Re-measured against HEAD `e6b96e3` in this session: eleven dispatch
renderings, eleven contract markers, **two** of them inside the OPEN arm (indices 1069 and 1321
on the Core fork today; both figures moved twice since the original 2026-08-17 review and will
move again after 11-07/11-08 land — re-derive rather than transcribe), and exactly **one**
`is.workflow.actions.askllm` on the Aware fork. A user who removes their bypass reaches the
Intention primitive with no contract audit at all, and there is nothing they can observe that
would tell them their fork just became Core. `docs/sentient_core_check.py:103` still asserts
`assert len(models) == 1`, which pins the defect rather than detecting it — unchanged since the
original review, across the three phases (12, 13, 16) that have since executed.

**One thing genuinely changed underneath this defect since the original draft, and it changes
this plan's Task 1 mechanically.** Phase 16 (plan 16-01) closed that plan's own WR-01 by making
`if_block()`'s `key` argument **required** rather than defaulted, precisely to stop two call
sites from silently deriving the same `uuid5` GroupingIdentifier. That fix is real and correct
— but `audit_block()` calls `if_block()` with fixed literal keys (`"enabled"`, `"circle-min"`,
...), so a second call to `audit_block()` today would supply the SAME literal keys and collide
just as surely as before the required-key change, only now by deliberate argument rather than by
omitted default. Making `key` required did not make a second insertion safe; it moved the
obligation from "remember to pass a key" to "remember the key must differ per rendering", which
this plan's discriminator must satisfy. Phase 16 also armed a general-purpose backstop for this
whole class, `verify_group_identifier_uniqueness()`, already in `build_sentient.py`'s verify
chain — this plan's insertion is proven against that guard too, not only against its own ad hoc
checks.

Purpose: make the fork's differentiator unconditional on any setting unrelated to it, and make
the count self-deriving so the next rendering cannot silently halve it again.
Output: one audit per OPEN-arm rendering, per-rendering identifiers threaded through every
`uid()` call including `if_block()`'s now-required key, and two checkers that derive rather than
pin.
</objective>

<gap3_resolution>
## Insert into every OPEN-arm rendering. The "deliberate product decision" option is rejected.

The verifier offered two legitimate resolutions and required one to be chosen and justified. This
reasoning is unchanged from the original 2026-08-17 draft — nothing in Phases 12, 13 or 16
altered the tradeoff — and is restated rather than re-derived.

**Rejected: record "audit only the Panic-Escape path" in `docs/CAPABILITY-DECISIONS.md` and
surface it in the Note.** Three reasons.

1. **It is not a statable product rule.** Written out honestly it reads: *if you remove the
   easy-exit bypass, you also lose the on-device intelligence audit.* Those two features have no
   relationship. A user cannot predict it, cannot want it, and cannot undo one without the other.
   Recording it would be documenting a defect in the vocabulary of a feature.
2. **It inverts the escalation.** The bypass-removed path is the *harder* path — the user has
   deliberately given up their exit and goes straight into the intervention. That is exactly the
   moment the contract audit is most useful, and the current behaviour removes it precisely
   there.
3. **It is the same silent-degradation class this phase's own guard was written to eliminate.**
   BD-06 Decision 5's coverage guard exists because two well-formed halves stopped agreeing with
   each other and produced no error. This is that shape one level up: a fork that silently
   becomes the other fork, with nothing on device to see.

**Chosen: insert into every OPEN-arm rendering, located structurally.** The cost is real and is
paid explicitly — `uid()` **and** `if_block()`'s key argument must both take a per-rendering
discriminator, because `audit_block()` calls both with fixed literal keys, and both are
deterministic hashes over that literal, so a second call with unchanged keys emits
byte-identical `GroupingIdentifier`s and `UUID`s regardless of whether the key argument is
optional or required. `.claude/CLAUDE.md` §4 names a reused `GroupingIdentifier` as the top
documented real-world failure mode in this toolchain, so that discriminator is not a tidiness
measure, it is the thing that makes the second block legal — and it is now checked twice: by
this plan's own uniqueness assertions, and by Phase 16's already-armed
`verify_group_identifier_uniqueness()`.

**Locating the OPEN arm structurally, rather than taking every marker in the file, is also a
choice and is deliberate.** The other nine markers are the Test-a-Circle submenu in the MANUAL
arm. Auditing those would put a model call behind a diagnostic menu item, which is neither the
fork's purpose nor safe to assume is cheap. The OPEN arm is located the same way
`verify_circle_zero_silence()` locates it — from the router's own OPEN literal test and its
grouping identifier — so a future rendering added anywhere inside that arm is covered without
another code change.
</gap3_resolution>
